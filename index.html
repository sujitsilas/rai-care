The Patch

A dressing that heals while you move

SparkHeal sits on the wound like any other dressing. Inside, a magnetoelastic generator turns the body’s own motion into gentle electrical stimulation, delivered directly to the wound bed.

+

Inside the patch

Two layers, one job: keep therapy running between clinic visits. In benchtop testing, output stayed within the physiologic range and below known electrical-stimulation safety thresholds.*

  • Self-powered. No batteries or wires.
  • Adjunct to standard care. Fits the dressing change clinics already do.
  • Activated by movement, even blood flow.*
  • Modulates pain without opioids.*

*Early signals from benchtop testing and initial human use. These have not been established in controlled clinical studies, and SparkHeal has not been cleared or approved by the FDA.

Why we built it

Meet Mrs. Smith.

Mrs. Smith has a foot ulcer that hasn’t closed in months. Every week she waits in a crowded clinic for a debridement and a fresh dressing, then goes home until the next visit. Between appointments, the dressing on her wound does what dressings have always done: it covers, and it waits.

Chronic wounds rarely stay solved. They shrink, recur and open in new places. Clinicians told us a diabetic foot ulcer is never cured, only put into remission. For patients like Mrs. Smith, the stakes are a limb.

We built SparkHeal so the dressing she already wears can do more. Every step she takes delivers therapy to the wound, between visits, with nothing new for her to do.

Mrs. Smith is a fictional character created for illustration, inspired by experiences patients and caregivers shared with us. Any resemblance to actual persons, living or dead, is purely coincidental. The photo is for illustration only and does not depict any person described here.

Clinical workflow

No new workflow. Just a better dressing.

SparkHeal goes on as the wound contact layer during the dressing change clinics already perform. The visit, the billing and the patient’s routine stay the same. What changes is what happens between visits.

  1. 1 Wound care specialist

    Assess & debride

    The MD or DPM evaluates the wound and debrides as usual.

    Unchanged
  2. 2 Clinician or nurse

    Dress the wound

    SparkHeal is applied as the contact layer, under the usual secondary dressing.

    SparkHeal applied
  3. 3 Patient at home

    Life goes on

    Everyday movement, like walking and shifting, powers stimulation between visits. Nothing to charge, wear or remember.

    Therapy between visits
  4. 4 Clinic or home health

    Next dressing change

    The old patch comes off with the dressing and a fresh one goes on.

    Same schedule

Repeats at every dressing change until the wound closes

FDA & regulatory

A de-risked path to clearance

Built with regulatory consultants and stress-tested during I-Corps with reimbursement experts.

  1. Obtained

    Non-significant risk designation

    An independent IRB (Advarra) designated SparkHeal non-significant risk, so early human studies can proceed without an IDE.

  2. Completed

    First in-human pilot

    Early human use under IRB oversight showed a localized analgesic effect, consistent with imaging.*

  3. Planned

Principles

How we work

Five commitments that shape every decision at RaiCare, from the bench to the bedside.

Healing That Moves With You™
  1. 01

    Patient First

    Patient outcomes are the measure of success at RaiCare. We listen to patients and understand their needs, experiences, and priorities first hand. Care should be built around the person receiving it, with attention to both healing and the experience of care.

  2. 02

    First Principles

    We approach problems from first principles. That means separating the underlying problem from the terminology, assumptions, and established workflows around it, making it modular and adaptable. Starting with what is known, we ask what actually needs to be true for a better solution to work.

  3. 03

    Scientific Rigor

    We hold our work to a high scientific standard. Ideas should be tested, challenged, and refined as evidence develops. Our goal is to contribute to the wound healing community by asking difficult questions, addressing gaps in understanding, and producing findings that can withstand careful scrutiny.

  4. 04

    Simplicity

    Good care should not require unnecessary complexity. We look for ways to make care easier for patients and simpler for the people delivering it, particularly when clinical workflows can become burdensome. Simplicity is not about removing rigor. It is about removing what does not need to be there.

  5. 05

    Curiosity

    Curiosity drives how we work. We ask questions even when the answers are not obvious, and we remain open to changing our thinking when new evidence emerges. The aim is continuous improvement, including progress that may come from unexpected directions.
